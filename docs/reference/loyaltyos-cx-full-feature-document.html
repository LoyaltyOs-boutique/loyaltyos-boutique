Part 0 · The logical spine

Why this document cannot fall apart

Every well-built system rests on a small set of fixed ideas, stated once, and then never contradicted. This part states them. Every feature in Part 2 and every guarantee in Part 3 exists because of one of these ideas — and every rule is tagged with the principle it serves, so a contradiction is impossible to hide. The traceability matrix in Part 6 shows the whole picture at a glance.

The eight operating principles

These are the axioms. If a proposed feature or rule conflicts with one of them, the feature or rule is wrong — not the principle.

P1 · Human authorityNothing reaches a client without your recorded approval.There is no code path that sends autonomously. Approval always records who and when. This is built into the system, not a policy written on top of it.
P2 · Memory with reasonsEvery conclusion the system draws about a client carries its reasons.If the system thinks "she likes pastels," it can show you the two purchases and five likes behind that. No black boxes, ever.
P3 · Your correction winsStated facts outrank inferences — permanently.What you tell the system beats what it guesses, and the system never silently reverts a correction. (See the single, explicit boundary in Part 1.)
P4 · Consent is sacredThree independent switches, re-checked at the moment of sending.Occasion wishes, marketing, and STOP are separate decisions, each checked again every single time — never assumed from an old list.
P5 · HonestyReal stock, real prices, honest counts.No padding a thin suggestion, no guessing an unreadable price, no dead links, no stale pages. When the truth is modest, the system says so.
P6 · DignityClients never see budget talk or anything that feels like a bill.Price-range and spend filtering happen invisibly; clients only ever see beautiful pieces and warm words.
P7 · The design is frozenNo visual drift, enforced by a machine — not goodwill.The half-built website design is never altered; an automated check rejects any work that touches it.
P8 · Graceful failureEvery feature fails politely.Every bad day has a fallback that is quieter, safer and more honest than the failure itself. Details in Part 4.

The vocabulary — every thing has one name

A system falls apart when two parts use two different words for the same thing. This is the fixed list of the things in this system, used identically everywhere from here on.

The consent model — three switches, kept apart

Conflating these is the single most common way a messaging system falls apart — and the most common way it breaks the law. They are three different decisions, stored separately, checked separately.

1 · Occasion wishesDoes this client want birthday and anniversary wishes? On by invitation, off otherwise. Checked before every occasion message.
2 · MarketingDoes this client want lookbooks, podcast shares, event invites and suggestions? A "no" quietly excludes her from every outreach — re-checked at the moment of sending, not when a list was built.
3 · STOPA hard, immediate opt-out. If a client writes "STOP" to the Studio number, every message to her stops at once, permanently, across all features.
Why three and not one: a client might love birthday wishes but refuse marketing; another might accept marketing but type STOP in a moment of irritation. One switch could not honour any of that. Three switches, each re-read at send time, can.

The law — what a public launch obliges

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, 2025 are fully operational since 14 November 2025. A public launch in India must honour them. This document states the obligations in plain words; the detailed compliance plan lives in the technical document.

  • Consent and notice. We tell each client, plainly, what we hold and why, and we process it on the consent switches above. P4
  • Rights. A client can ask what we hold, correct it, or ask to be erased — and we respond within ninety days. Erasure is real, not cosmetic (F1, F2). P2 · P3
  • Security. Client data is encrypted, access-controlled and masked where possible — a statutory duty, with penalties up to ₹250 crore for failure. P2
  • Retention. Data is kept only as long as it serves the purpose, then erased — while logs the law requires are retained. P5
  • Breach. If client data is ever compromised, we have a prepared, practised process to contain it and notify the Board and affected clients. P8
Part 1 · The client record

The foundation everything stands on

The previous document described the memory but never defined the four things that give it shape: points, tiers, the exclusive list, and the list/segment/tag model. They now have a single, fixed definition — because features like suggestions (F5) and events (F8) depend on them and must not improvise.

1.1 · Memory — facts, inferences, corrections

A perfect memory, with reasons attached

Each client's memory holds two kinds of thing: facts you state (birthday, anniversary, phone number, sizes) and inferences the system forms from behaviour (favourite designers, colours she keeps loving, her usual price range). Every inference stores its reasons. You can inspect, correct or delete any of it.

The rules

  • Every inference is visible with its reasons — "two purchases and five likes," never "the computer said so." P2
  • Your correction always wins over an inference, permanently. P3
  • The one boundary: the system learns from your edits to wording (how it phrases drafts), but never from your edits to facts. A stated fact is treated as final, not as a training example. P3
  • Private details (measurements, staff notes, internal taste notes) stay confidential and can never be reached through a client link — blocked by the system, not just hidden on a screen. P2
  • A client who asks to be forgotten can be properly, completely erased. DPDP
This single boundary resolves the one tension in the previous version: "the system learns from your edits" (good, for drafts) vs "your correction wins forever" (good, for facts). They are now two different rules about two different things, and neither can swallow the other.
1.2 · The loyalty account & points

Points: the mechanism is fixed, the numbers are yours

The loyalty account is the client's points ledger. The mechanism below is fixed and safe; the numbers (rates, values, thresholds) are parameters you set in Settings — they are listed in Part 7 as decisions to confirm, and changing them later never breaks the ledger.

The mechanism

  • Accrual: points are earned on every purchase — in-store and online at events — at a rate you set (e.g. 1 point per ₹100). P5
  • Gifts: points can also be given as a gift (the birthday gift in F6), at a value you set. P6
  • Value: points convert to store credit at a rate you set (e.g. 1 point = ₹1). P5
  • Redemption: points redeem at checkout, online and in-store, exactly the same way — the two are always in sync. P5
  • Honesty: the points history is append-only. It can never be edited or deleted — the one part of the record you cannot rewrite. You may add a note; you may not change money history. P5
Points are the load-bearing concept the previous document used everywhere and defined nowhere. Now every mention of "points" in Part 2 refers to exactly this ledger.
1.3 · Tiers & the exclusive list

Standard · Champagne · Noir — and the ~2,000

Every client has a tier: Standard (the default), Champagne, or Noir. The tier decides eligibility for lists, events and curation. The exclusive list — about 2,000 clients — is the specific group that may receive personal suggestions (F5).

The rules

  • Tier is decided by rules you set — typically trailing-twelve-month spend, optionally tenure — and the thresholds are yours (Part 7). P5
  • The exclusive list is yours to manage. The system can propose members from a rule (e.g. Noir, opted in to suggestions, active in the last year), but you add and remove people by hand, and your hand always wins. P3
  • No dignity leaks. A client is never shown her tier, her segment, or the word "exclusive list." These labels are internal only. P6
The previous document said the boutique "manages the list yourself" but never said what qualifies a client for it. Now it does: a rule proposes, you decide.
1.4 · Lists, segments & tags

Three tools for choosing an audience

When a feature sends to "a list," three distinct tools are in play, and each behaves differently at send time.

ToolWhat it isExample
Saved listA fixed set of clients you assembled once and named."Top 100 clients"
SegmentA live rule that resolves at the moment of sending."all Champagne clients"
TagA label on a client record, for ad-hoc grouping."wedding-season"

The rule that keeps them honest

  • Whatever tool you choose, the audience is re-resolved at the moment of sending — so a client who opted out, changed tier, or said STOP yesterday is excluded from today's send, even if the list was built last month. P4
Part 2 · The eight features

The features — each traced to its principles

Eight features, not eleven. The three sections the previous version counted as features — your WhatsApp habit, approval, editability — are reframed here as the guarantees in Part 3, because they are the frame the features hang on, not features themselves. Each feature below ends with the principles its rules serve.

F1 · The system's memory of every client

Remembering, safely

The system remembers facts and inferences about each client — birthdays, anniversaries, purchases, likes, favourite designers, sizes — visible only to the boutique, with reasons behind every guess.

The journey — a memory is born

  1. Meera becomes a client. Her name, number, birthday and anniversary are saved, with the occasion-wishes switch set.
  2. She browses her private link, taps ♥ on three Sabyasachi pieces, reserves one.
  3. The system notes it quietly: "likes Sabyasachi, size M, prefers pastels" — each note with its reasons attached.
  4. She buys; the purchase adds to her taste profile and price range, and to her points ledger.
  5. You ask "why does it think that?" and it shows the two purchases and five likes — never "the computer said so."
  6. You correct it — and your correction wins, permanently.

The rules

  • Occasion wishes only if the client has agreed — the switch is checked every time, never assumed. P4
  • Every memory has visible reasons. P2
  • Your corrections beat its guesses, always, and are never silently undone. P3
  • Measurements and staff notes stay confidential, blocked from client links. P2
  • A client can be properly erased on request. DPDP
F2 · The client's private link

No app, no password — just her link

Each client has one private link to her personal page: her points, her lookbooks, her invitations — in the design she already knows. It arrives on WhatsApp and remembers her, so likes and reservations are credited to her.

The journey — Meera opens her world

  1. She taps the link in a WhatsApp message — it opens like a site made just for her.
  2. She sees her points, lookbooks, invitations — same design she already knows.
  3. She taps hearts and reserves; you see it instantly on the dashboard.
  4. She changes phones, or a link leaks — one button issues a fresh link or switches the old one off, instantly.

The rules

  • Links are private and unguessable; guessing attacks are blocked automatically. P2
  • Switching off or renewing a link takes effect immediately. P8
  • Confidential information is unreachable through a client link — blocked by the system, not just the screen. P2
  • Old links expire gently — the client is offered a fresh one, not a dead page. P8
F3 · Building lookbooks

Every lookbook has a name; every picture its own details

Lookbooks are organised under a designer or a collection. Each picture is its own card with its own price, size and colour — two pieces in one book can differ entirely.

The journey — three ways to fill the shelf

  1. Start with a name: "Sabyasachi" or "The Wedding Edit 2026."
  2. PDF: upload a designer's PDF; each page becomes a card, and printed names/prices are read and filled in for you.
  3. Instagram link: paste the post; pictures and the designer's name arrive. If Instagram blocks the pull, it simply asks you to upload — no dead end.
  4. Manual: upload photos yourself, one or many.
  5. Every picture gets its card — price, size, colour; fill what you know, leave what you don't.
  6. You confirm. Nothing goes live until you press Publish.

The rules

  • Duplicate uploads are noticed — the system warns instead of duplicating. P5
  • The reading assistant never guesses a price it can't see clearly — it marks it "unreadable" for you to fill. P5
  • Everything published here becomes material the suggestion engine and events can use. —
  • Any card can be edited after publishing. P3
F4 · Sharing — a whole lookbook, or one piece

Every lookbook has a Share button — and so does every picture

Share the whole collection, or just the one lehenga a client asked about. Two ways: copy a link and paste it yourself, or send from the dashboard with the WhatsApp button.

The journey — two taps on WhatsApp

  1. Pick what to share — whole lookbook, or one piece (the piece's link shows its own details).
  2. Copy the link and paste it yourself, in your own words, from your own number — free and informal.
  3. Or use the WhatsApp button: a ready message with the picture appears; edit, pick a client or saved list, approve — sent.
  4. See what happens next: who opened, looked, loved, reserved — on the dashboard.
  5. At showtime, any lookbook becomes an event with one switch.

The rules

  • Copy-paste sharing is yours — your number, your style, no system approval needed. G1
  • Button-sharing goes through your approval, like everything the system sends. P1
  • A removed piece shows a polite "no longer available" with the rest of the collection — never a dead page. P8
  • If a piece sells out after the lookbook is shared, its card on the shared link updates live to "Sold out" — no stale price, no surprise at the counter. P5
  • Shared links always show current details; you can hide a price before sharing. P5
F5 · Custom suggestions — for the exclusive list

The system shops your shelves for each client

When you want to delight an exclusive client — or she asks — the system suggests a personal lookbook built only from what's actually in stock, matched to her designer, size and taste. You review, adjust, approve.

The journey — "anything new for wedding season?"

  1. Meera messages: "Anything new for wedding season? Love Sabyasachi." — or you open her page and press "Suggest Lookbook."
  2. The system shops your shelves: only in-stock pieces, her size, her usual price range, favourite designers on top.
  3. You see the draft: 6–10 pieces, each with a small reason. If only two truly fit, it says so honestly instead of padding.
  4. You adjust freely — swap, remove, change the note.
  5. Press Approve. Meera receives her private link.
  6. She loves three, reserves one. Next time, the draft is even more her.

The rules

  • Suggestions go only to clients on your exclusive list — the list you manage (Part 1.3). P3
  • Only real, in-stock items — checked again at the moment of sending. P5
  • Clients never see "budget" or "price range" — filtering is invisible; only beautiful pieces appear. P6
  • Out of stock before sending? Swapped or flagged to you — never sent as a disappointment. P5
  • What she likes and reserves is credited back, improving her taste profile. P2
F6 · Birthdays & anniversaries

The morning wish, ready before your chai

Every morning the system checks the calendar and puts a reminder on the dashboard — whose day is due — with a warm, personal message already drafted. You read, maybe change a word, and press Approve & Send.

The journey — the morning of Meera's birthday

  1. 9:00 AM — the system finds today's occasions: clients who agreed to wishes and haven't been wished this year.
  2. 9:01 AM — the dashboard shows the reminder with the message ready, including the gift you configured: "…we've added a little gift to your loyalty account. ✨"
  3. You read it. It sounds like you. Change a word — it's a normal text box.
  4. Press Approve & Send. It reaches Meera's WhatsApp.
  5. She replies "Thank you! 😍" — the reply appears on her card. If she ever replies STOP, the system never messages her again.

The rules

  • No message without your approval — built into the system, not a setting. P1
  • One wish per occasion per year, guaranteed — double-tapping Send can't duplicate it. P5
  • The AI chooses words, never facts: names exactly as in the profile; gifts only from what you configured. P5
  • If a draft fails quality checks, a safe classic version is prepared — there's always something good to send. P8
  • Wishes never mention spend or anything that feels like a bill — warmth first. P6
  • Missed a morning? Nothing sends itself and nothing is lost — the reminder waits. P1
F7 · Sharing your podcast & videos

Paste a link, pick who, press send

When a new episode or short is out, paste the YouTube link. A preview and a short message appear; you choose exactly who receives it — one client, a saved list, a segment, or everyone.

The journey — the new episode is out

  1. Paste the YouTube link. Title and thumbnail appear on their own.
  2. A short message is drafted underneath — edit it however you like.
  3. Pick the audience: one client, a saved list, a segment, or every contact.
  4. The system shows the reach — "This will go to 1,850 people."
  5. Press Approve. It lands as a neat message with the link; the dashboard shows received and read.

The rules

  • Lists are yours to build — hand-picked or rule-built (Part 1.4), saved and reusable. P3
  • Anyone who said "no marketing" is quietly left out — re-checked at send time, not when the list was made. P4
  • Messages go out in sensible hours only — never at 2 a.m. P6
  • Each person receives it exactly once, even for large sends. P5
  • Every send shows its results: delivered, read, replied. P5
F8 · Virtual events

The link that opens like a trunk show

A private, time-bound showcase: invited clients see a teaser the day before, and at the event hour prices appear for everyone at once. Each piece shows its own price, size and colour — clients can buy online or book with the stylist.

The journey — a festive trunk show

  1. Create the event: name it, pick the lookbook, set the live time and duration, choose the guest list.
  2. The day before, invites go out — the invite message waits for your approval, like everything else.
  3. Early birds see the teaser — collection shown beautifully, prices hidden.
  4. At the event hour it unlocks — prices appear for everyone at once, like doors opening.
  5. Meera falls for a lehenga. She can Buy Now (UPI or card; points added automatically) or Book with Stylist (size and colour preference; the stylist gets a WhatsApp alert within a minute).
  6. The dashboard shows it live: who visited, what's bought or booked, what's left. Sold-out pieces show "Sold out" on their own.
  7. After the window closes, the link ends gracefully; a gentle follow-up to browsers is possible — with your approval.

The rules

  • Prices are truly hidden before unlock — not sent to the visitor's device until the hour. P5
  • Online purchases create real orders with loyalty points, in sync with the shop counter. P5
  • Bookings create a reserved order and alert the stylist within a minute. P8
  • Stock is honest: what's sold shows as sold, live. P5
  • A stuck payment waits politely as "confirming" — no double charges — and offline booking always works. P8
  • Event links expire when the event ends — no stale pages. P5
Part 3 · The four standing guarantees

The frame the features hang on

These are not features — they are the standing conditions under which every feature operates. They were scattered as "features 9, 10 and 11" in the previous version; they are now one fixed frame, each with its own rules, and nothing in Part 2 may contradict them.

G1 · Your number, your habits — untouched

Two doors, zero interference

The boutique already messages clients its own way, from the Owner's number, in its own style. That continues exactly as it is. The new system works from the Studio number, so the two never share queues, limits or problems.

The rules

  • The Owner's number and tool are never migrated, re-registered or "upgraded" — the team is not allowed to touch them. P7
  • The Studio number's sends never share a queue with your broadcasts — one can never slow or block the other. P8
  • Clients who reply STOP to the Studio number are respected immediately and quietly. P4
  • All system messages use approved WhatsApp formats — properly branded, never spam. P5
  • During building, test messages go to test numbers only; real clients are reached only after you switch the system on. P1
G2 · You approve everything

A human button on every send

No client-facing message is ever sent without your recorded approval — who, what, when. It is not a policy; it is how the system is built.

The rules

  • There is no code path that sends without an approval. P1
  • Approvals record who and when — full traceability, always. P1
  • You can edit anything before approving, and the system learns from your wording edits — while your factual corrections always win (Part 1.1). P3
  • Every feature has its own off-switch in Settings — turn one off; the rest keep running. P8
G3 · The design is frozen

The look you love never changes

The website design you already love is never altered by the building team. New features appear inside the existing look.

The rules

  • Every piece of work is checked for design changes before it can be accepted; design drift is rejected automatically. P7
  • New features appear inside the existing design — never instead of it. P7
G4 · Everything is editable by you

No black boxes

Every input the system depends on — memories, wording, gifts, drafts, lookbook details, lists, timings, links — is data you can see and change from the dashboard, anytime, without calling anyone.

The rules

  • Every change is recorded: who changed what, when, and what it was before. P5
  • Your edits outrank the system's conclusions, always. P3
  • Two things are intentionally not editable: the loyalty points history and the record of past sends — money and history stay honest. You may add a note; you cannot rewrite history. P5
  • "Editable" never includes the website design — that protection stands above everything (G3). P7
Part 4 · When things go wrong

The system fails politely

Every feature has a plan for its bad day. In every case, the fallback is quieter, safer and more honest than the failure. This is principle P8 made concrete.

The AI writes something oddQuality checks catch it; a safe, classic message is prepared instead. You always have something good to send.
A piece goes out of stockSwapped or flagged before a lookbook is sent; during an event it shows "Sold out" live.
WhatsApp rejects a formatThe system falls back to the last approved format and tells you — messages keep flowing.
A client says STOPAll messaging to her stops immediately and quietly.
You double-press SendImpossible. Each message can only go once.
Instagram or YouTube blocks a fetchThe system asks you to upload or paste the details manually — no dead ends.
An event payment gets stuckThe order waits as "confirming" without double-charging; the stylist booking path always works.
Any part misbehavesIts off-switch in Settings stops it instantly; the rest of the boutique keeps running.
A link leaks or a phone changesOne button issues a fresh link or switches the old one off, instantly.
Client data is compromisedA prepared breach process contains it and notifies the Board and affected clients, as the law requires.
Part 5 · The build & launch sequence

How this gets built — and what must be true before launch

Piece by piece, each finished, tested and shown to you before it touches anything real. Then, before anything goes public, a fixed set of gates must all pass.

The build order, in plain steps

  1. The memory — learn to remember clients, safely and correctly. Nothing visible changes yet.
  2. The birthday engine — morning reminders and drafted wishes, tested with sample clients first.
  3. The WhatsApp plumbing — the Studio number set up on its own, with test numbers throughout.
  4. The private links — clients receive their personal pages.
  5. The lookbook shelf — PDF, Instagram and manual creation, with per-piece price, size and colour.
  6. Custom suggestions — the exclusive list starts receiving curated lookbooks.
  7. Sharing & the podcast section — links, WhatsApp buttons, lists and segments.
  8. Virtual events — invitations, unlocks, online buying and stylist bookings.

The launch gates

A launch fails not when a feature breaks, but when a principle breaks unnoticed. So each gate below verifies a principle, not just a button. All must pass before the first real client is reached.

1Consent is live. All three switches exist, and a test confirms that a client who opted out yesterday is excluded from today's send. P4
2Approval is recorded. A test confirms no message can send without a recorded who-and-when. P1
3The Studio number is verified with WhatsApp-approved templates, on test numbers only. P5
4The design check is on. An automated test confirms any visual drift is rejected. P7
5The points ledger is append-only and reconciles with the shop counter. P5
6Erasure works. A test client is fully deleted, and their link dies with them. DPDP
7Every off-switch works. Turn each feature off; confirm the rest keep running. P8
8A pilot ran. A small, friendly list goes live first; you watch it for a few days before the full public launch. P8

One sentence to remember it by

The system never forgets a client, never sends without your approval, never changes the design you love — and everything it knows, you can edit; the only things you cannot rewrite are money and history.

How to keep this document alive: after any change, re-read the traceability matrix (Part 6). If a new rule serves no principle, cut it; if a change weakens a principle's home, the matrix will show the hole. That single habit is what keeps the architecture from ever falling apart.
